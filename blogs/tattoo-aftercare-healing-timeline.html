Tattoo Aftercare: A Day-by-Day Healing Timeline

Aftercare is half of how a tattoo turns out. Here is what normal healing looks like, what to do, and what to avoid.

Home  /  Tattoo Blog  /  Aftercare

By The Kala Culture Tattoos  ·  Updated 10 October 2026  ·  2 min read

A good tattoo can still heal badly without proper care. Healing varies from person to person, and artists use different wraps and products, so always follow the instructions your own artist gives you first. Use this guide to understand what is normal.

Day 0 to 2: fresh and tender

The tattoo will be red, a little swollen and sore, and it may ooze a clear or slightly coloured fluid. Your artist will cover it and tell you when to take the wrap off. After that, wash your hands, rinse the tattoo gently with lukewarm water and a mild, unscented soap, pat it dry with a clean paper towel and let it air dry for a few minutes.

Day 3 to 7: dry, itchy and flaky

The tattoo starts to dry and flake, and it can itch. This is normal. Apply a thin layer of the moisturiser or ointment your artist recommended. Do not scratch or pick at flakes, because that can pull ink out and leave patches.

Week 2: peeling settles

Most of the flaking ends and the surface looks a bit cloudy or dull. This is the skin healing over the ink, and the colours often look softer for a while.

Weeks 3 to 4: the surface heals

For most tattoos the top layer of skin has healed by now. The tattoo looks clearer and the shine fades.

Months 2 to 3: fully settled

Deeper layers of skin keep settling for a few months. This is when you see the final result. If any spots look patchy, ask your artist about a touch-up.

What to do

  • Wash your hands before you touch the tattoo.
  • Keep it clean, using lukewarm water and mild soap.
  • Use only a thin layer of moisturiser. Too much can clog the skin.
  • Wear loose, clean, breathable clothes over it.
  • Drink water and get enough sleep. Healing is slower when you are run down.

What to avoid

  • Scratching or picking at scabs and flakes.
  • Soaking in swimming pools, the sea or long baths for about the first two weeks.
  • Direct sun and tanning beds while it heals.
  • Tight clothes that rub the area.
  • Heavy sweating in the first few days, especially if the tattoo is where clothes rub.
  • Thick layers of ointment.

Sun care once it has healed

Sun fades ink faster than anything else. Once the tattoo has healed, use sunscreen of SPF 30 or higher when it is exposed. This matters most for colour tattoos and thin fine line work.

When to see a doctor

Some redness and tenderness are normal in the first days. See a doctor if you notice redness that keeps spreading, increasing pain, swelling, heat, pus, a bad smell or a fever.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is peeling normal after a tattoo?

Yes. Light flaking and itching usually start a few days in and settle by the end of the second week. Do not pick or scratch it, because that can pull ink out.

Can I swim after getting a tattoo?

Avoid swimming pools, the sea and long baths for about the first two weeks, because soaking can irritate the healing skin. Short showers are fine, and your artist may give you more specific advice.

When can I exercise after a tattoo?

Light movement is usually fine after a day or two. Avoid heavy sweating and anything that rubs the tattoo for the first few days, and follow your artist's advice for your spot.

What should I put on a new tattoo?

Use the moisturiser or ointment your artist recommends, and keep it to a thin layer. Too much product can clog the skin and slow healing.
